# Writing a level 1 (Outcome) artifact

Lessons from the first Outcome reviews (2026-10-27). Apply them when writing a level 1; the review prompt checks them too.

1. Use one term per concept. Define a term only when a reader couldn't tell what result it means; exactly what counts as each term is worked out at later levels.
2. Keep out anything that isn't the Outcome's responsibility, including vocabulary for it.
3. Every "achieves" claim has a scenario whose check would fail without it, and every scenario traces to a claim.
4. A check can't be satisfiable by the work it judges.
5. Definitions must not contradict scenarios.
6. Review the artifact on its own, not against the discussion that produced it.
7. Be simple and to the point. Say only what the reader needs to know what result is asked for, in short sentences. Reuse the same phrase for the same idea, and state what each check confirms, not how it is built.
8. Self-check before review. Fix a short vocabulary and use only those words. List the claims, scenarios and measures. Confirm every part of each claim has a scenario that would fail if that part were broken, each scenario and measure traces to a claim, no check or measure can be passed by the work it judges, every scenario states its own starting state, and every exception in a claim appears in each scenario and measure it touches. Wording problems are fixed here, not found in review.
9. State what the user gets. Leave exactly what counts and how it works to later levels. Different readings are expected at this level; later levels narrow them. Ambiguity matters only when no reader would know what to do.
10. A scenario sets its own starting state. Never rely on the work being judged to reach that state.
11. Carry every allowance or exception in a claim (for example "a question for clarity is fine") into each scenario and measure it touches. No check or measure may count against behaviour a claim requires or allows.
12. Fix a finding by removing the wording that caused it, not by adding a new qualifier or distinction. A new distinction becomes something every other claim, scenario and measure has to account for.
